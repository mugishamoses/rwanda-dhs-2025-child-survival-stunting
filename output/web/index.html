From 196 to 36: Rwanda saved its children. Now it must help them grow.

Seven national surveys, 1992 to 2025, on the first five years of life: who survives, who grows, and where the next gains must come from.

01 · SURVIVAL

We kept them alive

In 2000, about 196 of every 1,000 babies died before their fifth birthday. Today it is 36: 160 more children in every 1,000 grow past age five.

02 · CARE

Care reached every mother

Births moved into health facilities, antenatal visits multiplied, and families gained control over when to have children.

03 · THE COHORT

Follow 1,000 babies born today

Almost all now survive. But one in four survivors does not grow as they should, and the poorest families carry most of that weight.

04 · WHERE

Where children are stunted

Each district is raised by its stunting rate. Kigali sits low; the North and West rise highest. Nyarugenge (8.7%) is the lowest; Gicumbi is 4.5 times higher. Only Nyarugenge and Kicukiro are below the 15% target for 2029.

    06 · TIMELINE

    Faster, or we miss 2029

    Rwanda's NST2 plan aims for 15% stunting by 2029. At the pace of the last five years, the country reaches about 23%.

    Feed variety after 6 monthsOnly 27% of children aged 6–23 months get a minimum acceptable diet.
    Watch growth from birth to 2Stunting doubles between 6 months and 2 years.
    Keep girls in schoolChildren of mothers with more than secondary schooling are 6.5 times less often stunted.
    Focus on the North and WestGicumbi, Burera, Ngororero, Rutsiro and Karongi have the highest rates.
